# Hot Button Questions

Reference answers to frequently contested election questions, with official and research sources. Compiled for the VB Election Desk knowledge base (one-off addition, 2026-09-14).

## 2020 presidential election

Joe Biden won the 2020 presidential election. The official Electoral College result was 306 votes for Biden and 232 for Donald Trump. States certified their results, Congress counted the electoral votes, and objections to the electoral votes from Arizona and Pennsylvania were not sustained. Claims that fraud or irregularities changed the outcome have been extensively examined, but no credible evidence has established fraud sufficient to overturn the result.

**Sources**

- Official 2020 Electoral College results (National Archives): https://www.archives.gov/electoral-college/2020
- Stanford research examining prominent claims of fraud: https://humsci.stanford.edu/feature/debunking-evidence-election-fraud-cases

## Jan. 6 and presidential certification

On Jan. 6, 2021, a mob breached the U.S. Capitol while Congress was meeting to count the electoral votes from the 2020 presidential election, forcing Congress to suspend the proceeding. The attack occurred amid false claims that the election had been stolen and that Vice President Mike Pence or Congress had authority to reject valid electoral votes.

Congress subsequently changed the law governing the electoral count. The Electoral Count Reform Act of 2022 makes explicit that the vice president's role is ministerial, raises the threshold required for members of Congress to object to a state's electoral votes, limits the grounds for objections, and clarifies procedures for resolving disputes over electors. Those changes close several avenues that were invoked in the effort to overturn the 2020 result. They cannot, however, guarantee that nobody will attempt political violence or interfere with the process in the future.

**Sources**

- Electoral Count Reform Act (S. 4573, 117th Congress): https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/senate-bill/4573
- Broader collection of records and reporting about Jan. 6 (Lawfare, The Jan. 6 Project): https://www.lawfaremedia.org/projects-series/archived-projects/the-jan.-6-project

## Can voting machines be hacked?

Voting machines are computers, and like other computer systems they can have vulnerabilities. But the existence of a vulnerability, or even a demonstration that a machine could theoretically be compromised, is not evidence that votes in an actual election were changed.

Election security does not depend on assuming that voting equipment is impossible to hack. Voting systems are protected through layers of controls that can include testing and certification, pre-election accuracy testing, restrictions on physical and electronic access, chain-of-custody procedures, ballot reconciliation and post-election audits. Paper ballots and voter-verifiable paper records also allow election officials to check machine-generated totals independently. Audit requirements and procedures vary by state.

**Sources**

- U.S. Election Assistance Commission on voting-system security: https://www.eac.gov/are-voting-systems-secure
- U.S. Election Assistance Commission on election audits: https://www.eac.gov/election-officials/election-audits-across-united-states
- MIT Election Lab on election auditing: https://electionlab.mit.edu/research/projects/mapping-election-science/white-papers/election-auditing
- MIT Election Lab on voting technology: https://electionlab.mit.edu/research/voting-technology

## Are noncitizens voting in large numbers?

No. Noncitizens are prohibited from voting in federal elections, and there is no evidence that noncitizens are voting illegally in large numbers. Individual cases can and do occur, but available research finds them to be rare.

Peer-reviewed research has also examined claims of widespread noncitizen voting. A 2015 study in *Electoral Studies* found that estimates of substantial noncitizen voting based on survey data could be explained by the small amount of measurement error that is unavoidable when trying to measure an extremely rare event. A separate 2018 study using aggregate election data found no evidence supporting claims of widespread or systematic noncitizen voting. More recent reviews of documented cases likewise find very small numbers compared with the number of votes cast.

**Sources**

- Brookings overview of the evidence: https://www.brookings.edu/articles/why-do-so-few-noncitizens-try-to-vote/
- Ansolabehere, Luks and Schaffner, *Electoral Studies* (2015): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0261379415001420
- Cottrell, Herron and Westwood, *Electoral Studies* (2018): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S026137941730166X
