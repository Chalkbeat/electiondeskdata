About this manual 
This manual explains how you can efficiently perform your duties while providing 
excellent service to voters visiting the polling place. You will find information about 
specific procedures along with step-by-step Job Card instructions for setting up a safe 
and accessible polling place, processing voters during voting hours, and securing and 
transferring materials after the polls close. 
If you have a question that has not been addressed in this manual, please call your 
regional office (contact information is on the back of this manual). We are committed to 
supporting you throughout the election cycle and look forward to working with you on 
Election Day.  
Thank you in advance for devoting your valuable time and energy to serving as a poll 
worker. Your decision to serve in this vital role, to help maintain the integrity of 
elections, and to instill confidence in its outcomes, is appreciated.

Fundamentals – Campaigning 
Campaigning is not allowed within 200 feet of any entrance into the voting area during 
the hours the polls are open. These rules apply to voters, Poll Watchers, and Poll 
Workers. If you see a voter not following this rule, ask them to remove the campaigning 
material. If the voter will not comply, make a note of the event on the Precinct Incident 
Report, including the voter’s name, and take a photo. Provide this to the election 
supervisor, who will refer the information to the Department of Law.

Fundamentals – Poll Watchers 
A Poll Watcher is a person assigned by a political party, candidate, organization, or 
organized group that sponsors or opposes a ballot measure to observe activities at the 
polling place, guarding against illegal voting, fraudulent counting of ballots, and other 
election law violations. If you encounter any issues with a poll watcher or have further 
questions regarding the conduct of a poll watcher, please contact your regional 
supervisor. The morning of Election Day, designate an area next to the precinct register 
for poll watchers to remain throughout the day. Identify this area to any poll watchers.  
Poll Watchers MUST: 
• Poll watchers must wear a Poll Watcher badge with their name on the front 
and who they represent on the back (party, candidate, organization, or 
group). 
• Only one Poll Watcher from each political party, candidate, organization, or group is allowed in 
the polling place at a time. 
• Poll Watchers may stand close enough to hear and record the name of the person voting while 
maintaining voter privacy. The official who is checking in voters on the precinct register must 
clearly say the voter’s names out loud.  
• Poll watchers must NOT handle any voting materials or sit behind the poll worker’s table. 
• Poll Watchers may challenge a voter’s qualifications. 
• Poll watchers may observe the set-up procedures and check that the ballot box is empty before 
the polls open. They may closely observe the election officials as official ballots are prepared for 
distribution. 
• Poll watchers may remain in the polling place until all procedures are completed and in precincts 
where a scanner or tablet is used, a watcher may request an additional copy of the results tape. 
• In those precincts where hand counting of the ballots is completed, a poll watcher may observe 
the counting of the ballots. For rank choice general elections, only the first choice will be counted 
by the election board on election night.

Fundamentals – Exit Pollsters 
An Exit Pollster conducts a survey for news coverage or trends used to predict the 
outcome of an election. 
Exit Pollsters MUST: 
• Wear an Exit Pollster badge with the name of the person conducting the poll and the 
division staff authorization. 
• Conduct exit polling outside the polling place entrance. The campaign rule does not 
apply to exit polling. 
• NOT disrupt the voting. 
• ONLY approach voters who have already voted and wish to participate in the exit 
survey. 
• NOT block the entrance for voting.

Disability Awareness and Voter Assistance 
Information 
General Awareness Tips 
• Treat people with disabilities respectfully and as individuals first. 
• Be sensitive and aware of their needs; they are often more independent than 
perceived. 
• Limit inquiries about disabilities—focus on the business at hand. 
• Avoid assumptions; what works for one may not work for another. 
• Offer assistance, but wait for them to ask if they need help. 
• Use respectful terminology: prefer “people with disabilities” and avoid outdated 
terms. 
• Voters can have a chosen assistant unless they are their employer or a union 
agent.  
• Service animals are permitted.  
• Sample ballots can help voters prepare while waiting in line.  
• Provide extra time for tasks and the opportunity to vote unassisted with 
accessible tools.  
• Approach confused individuals courteously and ask specific questions to clarify 
assistance needed.  
• Exercise patience and provide concise explanations; voting can be new for 
many.  
Specific Needs 
Hearing Impairments: 
• Speak clearly and directly, avoiding exaggerated speech.  
• Use paper and pen if needed for communication.  
• If present with an interpreter, speak to the individual, not the interpreter.  
• To get attention, gently tap their shoulder or wave.  
Visual Impairments:  
• Identify yourself and provide verbal descriptions of the environment.  
• Describe surroundings using “clock” references if applicable.  
• Offer to guide only after they accept. Let them hold your arm.  
• Do not interact with service animals while they are working.  
• Keep paths clear and inform others of any obstacles, such as snow or ice.

• Never leave someone blind in a space; provide a point of reference.  
 
By following these guidelines, you can assist voters with disabilities effectively and 
respectfully.

Chairperson Reminders 
Prior to Election Day: 
• Recruit 3-4 workers. Make sure they are politically balanced. 
• Post notices if applicable.  
• Contact the polling place before Election Day to arrange for building access. 
• Verify that all equipment has been delivered (i.e., booths, ballot box, vote here 
signs, tables, and chairs). 
• Verify ballot stub numbers to the ballot receipt. 
• Store ballots and voting equipment in a safe, secure, and dry location. 
• Verify the parking lot is accessible with no obstructions. Walk from the parking lot 
to the voting booths to ensure there are no obstructions. Contact your election 
supervisor with any concerns. 
• Remind all workers to arrive at least 30 minutes before the polls open and set up 
as much as you can the day prior to avoid last-minute stress. 
Election Day: 
• By law, all equipment, materials, and notices must be ready for voters by 7:00 
am for Primary and General Elections. For state-conducted school board 
elections (REAA) and state-conducted special local elections, polls open at 8:00 
am.   
• Assign the voting job tasks and roles. Usually, four poll workers work at the 
polling place and may rotate duties. Job tasks are assigned per person and 
opening and closing tasks can be given to one or more people depending on the 
number of workers working. 
Final Checks at the End of the Night: 
• At 7:45 pm, announce that the polls will close in 15 minutes. 
• At 8:00 pm, declare the polls closed and proceed to close the door. If, due to the 
polling location, you can’t close the door, have a poll worker stand in line to 
inform late arrivals that the polls are closed. Allow people still in line at 8:00 pm to 
vote.   
• Review and sign: 
o Precinct Register Oath 
o Questioned Voter Register Oath 
o Special Needs Register 
o Language Assistance Log 
o Timesheets

Opening Task 4 – Accessible Voting Booths 
Step 1: Lift the legs, straighten the diagonal brace, and set the booth upright.  
Step 2: Open the booth by inserting a pen or similar object and sliding the metal locks 
towards the center of the case.  
Step 3: There should be two metal feet for each accessible -voting booth. The feet are 
left and right specific. One at a time, place the short metal stubs on a foot inside the 
tubing that forms a leg of the booth. Rotate the flat metal end pieces and hook them to 
the metal stops attached to the booth’s 
legs. Extend the curtain arms on each 
support to the front and straighten the 
diagonal braces. 
Step 4: Position the lid of the case so that 
the end of the booth’s upright curtain supports first through the holes in the short side of 
the lid. The long side of the lid will hang down the back of the booth. There are 1 ½-inch 
screws protruding from the long side of the lid. Push these screws through small holes 
in the upright curtain supports. 
Step 5: Hang the white curtain around the sides and back of the booth. Fasten the 
curtain to the extended curtain arms using the spring clips attached to the top of the 
curtain. 
Step 6: The door curtain has a metal rod with ends that are bent at right angles. Insert 
the ends of the curtain rod into the small openings at the end of each curtain arm at the 
front of the booth.

Election Day Role 3 – Ballot Issuer 
Goal: Hand voters the correct ballot 
 
You Will: 
�� Confirm voter signed the precinct register 
�� Hand the voter a ballot and give brief instructions 
�� Direct the voter to a booth 
Important: 
��� Track ballots issued (log or device) 
��� Never pre-mark a ballot 
Collect the SIGNED REGISTER card from the voter. 
Step 1: Issue the voter a paper ballot unless the voter needs or would benefit using the 
voting tablet. If the voter is going to use the voting tablet, direct them to the Voting 
Tablet Worker for assistance.  
Step 2: For regular paper ballots, you will issue them in numerical order, starting with 
the lowest stub number first. Provide the ballot to the voter inside a secrecy sleeve 
folder. 
Step 3: Provide instructions to the voter: 
• Vote both sides of the ballot, if applicable. 
• Completely fill in the ovals. 
Ranked Choice Voting General Election Only: Voter ranks as many or as few 
candidates as they like by completely filling in the oval in the (1st Choice, 2nd Choice, 
3rd Choice, etc.) column for each candidate.  The voter can only fill in one oval for each 
candidate or column.  
Step 4: Have a voter deposit the ballot into the precinct scanner or ballot box. 
WRITE-IN NOTE & REMINDER 
(GENERAL ELECTION and REAA ELECTION ONLY) 
Voter must write the name of the write-in candidate on the space provided AND 
completely fill in the oval to rank their choice. A list of write-in candidates will be 
included in supplies for poll workers to assist voters. Do NOT post the write-in list or 
discuss write-in candidates. Alaska does not allow presidential write-in candidates.

SPOILED BALLOTS 
If a voter makes an error on their ballot, the voter may receive a replacement ballot. A 
voter may only be issued up to two replacement ballots of any combination (paper or 
voting tablet).  
If the voter spoils all three ballots (original plus the two replacement ballots), the voter 
may receive a fourth and final ballot and cast it as a Questioned Ballot.  
On the fourth and final ballot, if the voter makes another error and needs to make a final 
correction, inform the voter that they cannot receive any more replacement ballots but 
can make the correction directly on the final ballot.  
To correct the error(s) on the final ballot, the voter will cross off their error(s) and write 
“No” next to the error(s), then fill in the desired oval and write “Yes” next to it. 
When done voting, instruct the voter to place and seal their final (fourth) ballot inside 
their completed Question Oath and Affidavit ballot envelope. The voter MUST also 
complete and sign the Questioned Register. You will need to cross off the voter’s 
signature on the precinct register and make a note next to the voter’s signature “voted a 
question ballot” since they are now a questioned voter. 
Step 1: Record the spoiled ballot, by marking “your initials” in the appropriate numbered 
box. (Mark the boxes in numerical order) on the “Spoiled Ballot Envelope Log”.  
Step 2: Instruct the voter to tear in half the spoiled ballot and place it into the Spoiled 
Ballot Envelope.  
Step 3: Issue the voter a new ballot. Only issue one replacement ballot at a time.

Who can assist a voter? 
Anyone can aid a voter except for a candidate for office in the election, the voter’s 
employer or an agent of that employer, or an officer or agent of the voter’s union. The 
person helping may go into the voting booth to assist the voter.  
Can I Help?

Language Assistance 
Refer to the Election Procedures for Bilingual Election Workers handbook. Use the 
Language Assistance Requests Log to record the type of assistance provided and in 
what language. Do not list voter’s name. 
Sign and return the Language Assistance Log even if you did not have any voters who

Need a translator? 
Contact your election supervisor. In many rural areas, at least one member of the 
election board is bilingual. 
When providing an oral translation of the ballot information, the translator may not voice 
their own opinion on candidates or issues appearing on the ballot. 
Language assistance is available by calling our toll-free number: 1-866-954-86

Emergency Procedures 
Paper Ballot Supply: Not Enough or Missing 
What if the precinct does not have enough paper ballots or if ballots are missing? 
Contact the regional election supervisor immediately. If there are not enough ballots or 
ballots are missing on Election Day, use sample ballots, ballots removed from an official 
election pamphlet, or sheets of paper on which the names of candidates and issues are 
written, which can also be used until new ballots are delivered. Complete the certificate 
on the precinct register. (Keep in mind that you may also have the voter vote on the 
voting tablet unit until paper ballots are supplied.) 
Precinct Register Missing 
What should the poll workers do if the precinct register is missing? 
Contact the election supervisor immediately. Do not turn voters away from the polls. 
Have all voters vote a questioned ballot until a precinct register can be provided. 
• Ask each voter to sign in on the questioned voter register. If all lines are filled, 
have voters sign in on a separate sheet of paper. Voters must print and sign their 
names and provide identification.  
• Ask each voter to complete a questioned ballot envelope.  
If a precinct runs out of questioned ballot envelopes, voters may still vote. A plain 
envelope may be used. Ask voters to print their name, residence,  mailing address, and 
an identifier, such as a social security number, date of birth, or voter number, on the 
envelope. The voter must sign the envelope, and a poll worker must sign the envelope 
as a witness.  
• Ask the voter for identification. If the voter does not have identification, write “no 
ID” on the envelope.  
• Issue the voter a ballot and a secrecy sleeve or a piece of paper to return the 
ballot. The voter should be instructed to return the ballot to the poll worker in the 
sleeve or wrapped in paper after voting.  
• When the voter returns the voted ballot, it should be placed in the questioned 
ballot envelope or a plain envelope that has been written on and signed by the 
voter and a poll worker. Seal the envelope and have the voter drop the envelope 
in the ballot box.  
• When the election workers open the ballot box after the polls close, DO NOT 
open these envelopes. Rubber band the ballot envelopes together. Put them in 
the envelope for questioned ballots and mail or return to the election supervisor.  
 
63 
 
Polling Place Not Available 
What should the poll workers do if the polling place is unavailable? 
Find another polling place. If a school, public building, church or even a private home is 
nearby, ask permission to set up the polling place.  Post signs at both the original and 
new polling places so that voters can find the new location. 
As soon as possible, contact the election supervisor. The election supervisor will notify 
the voters and the news media of the change.  
Ballot Box Missing or Unusable 
What should the poll workers do if the ballot box is missing or unusable? 
A cardboard box or a similar empty box can be used as a temporary ballot box. Tape 
the box closed.  Cut a slot in the top to make an opening large enough for ballots to be 
inserted.  
Fire Alarm 
What should the poll workers do if a fire alarm goes off? 
Assess the situation; remember the safety of the poll workers comes first.  If possible, 
secure the ballots and lock the door as you leave. However, do not take any risk that 
may jeopardize the safety of your election workers.  
Tsunami Warning 
What should the poll workers do if a Tsunami Warning is issued? 
Follow evacuation instructions for your community provided by the public safety 
advisory for tsunami warnings. Assess the situation. Safety of your poll workers comes 
first. If possible, secure the ballots and lock the door as you leave. However, do not take 
any risk that may jeopardize the safety of your poll workers.

Fundamentals – Poll Watchers 
A Poll Watcher is a person assigned by a political party, candidate, organization, or 
organized group that sponsors or opposes a ballot measure to observe activities at the 
polling place, guarding against illegal voting, fraudulent counting of ballots, and other 
election law violations. If you encounter any issues with a poll watcher or have further 
questions regarding the conduct of a poll watcher, please contact your regional 
supervisor. The morning of Election Day, designate an area next to the precinct register 
for poll watchers to remain throughout the day. Identify this area to any poll watchers.  
Poll Watchers MUST: 
• Poll watchers must wear a Poll Watcher badge with their name on the front 
and who they represent on the back (party, candidate, organization, or 
group). 
• Only one Poll Watcher from each political party, candidate, organization, or group is allowed in 
the polling place at a time. 
• Poll Watchers may stand close enough to hear and record the name of the person voting while 
maintaining voter privacy. The official who is checking in voters on the precinct register must 
clearly say the voter’s names out loud.  
• Poll watchers must NOT handle any voting materials or sit behind the poll worker’s table.

Disability Awareness and Voter Assistance 
Information 
General Awareness Tips 
• Treat people with disabilities respectfully and as individuals first. 
• Be sensitive and aware of their needs; they are often more independent than 
perceived. 
• Limit inquiries about disabilities—focus on the business at hand. 
• Avoid assumptions; what works for one may not work for another. 
• Offer assistance, but wait for them to ask if they need help. 
• Use respectful terminology: prefer “people with disabilities” and avoid outdated 
terms. 
• Voters can have a chosen assistant unless they are their employer or a union 
agent.  
• Service animals are permitted.  
• Sample ballots can help voters prepare while waiting in line.  
• Provide extra time for tasks and the opportunity to vote unassisted with 
accessible tools.  
• Approach confused individuals courteously and ask specific questions to clarify 
assistance needed.  
• Exercise patience and provide concise explanations; voting can be new for 
many.  
Specific Needs 
Hearing Impairments: 
• Speak clearly and directly, avoiding exaggerated speech.  
• Use paper and pen if needed for communication.  
• If present with an interpreter, speak to the individual, not the interpreter.  
• To get attention, gently tap their shoulder or wave.  
Visual Impairments:  
• Identify yourself and provide verbal descriptions of the environment.  
• Describe surroundings using “clock” references if applicable.  
• Offer to guide only after they accept. Let them hold your arm.  
• Do not interact with service animals while they are working.  
• Keep paths clear and inform others of any obstacles, such as snow or ice.

• Never leave someone blind in a space; provide a point of reference.  
 
By following these guidelines, you can assist voters with disabilities effectively and 
respectfully.

SPOILED BALLOTS 
If a voter makes an error on their ballot, the voter may receive a replacement ballot. A 
voter may only be issued up to two replacement ballots of any combination (paper or 
voting tablet).  
If the voter spoils all three ballots (original plus the two replacement ballots), the voter 
may receive a fourth and final ballot and cast it as a Questioned Ballot.  
On the fourth and final ballot, if the voter makes another error and needs to make a final 
correction, inform the voter that they cannot receive any more replacement ballots but 
can make the correction directly on the final ballot.

“No” next to the error(s), then fill in the desired oval and write “Yes” next to it.

question ballot” since they are now a questioned voter.

Emergency Procedures 
Paper Ballot Supply: Not Enough or Missing 
What if the precinct does not have enough paper ballots or if ballots are missing? 
Contact the regional election supervisor immediately. If there are not enough ballots or 
ballots are missing on Election Day, use sample ballots, ballots removed from an official 
election pamphlet, or sheets of paper on which the names of candidates and issues are 
written, which can also be used until new ballots are delivered. Complete the certificate 
on the precinct register. (Keep in mind that you may also have the voter vote on the 
voting tablet unit until paper ballots are supplied.) 
Precinct Register Missing 
What should the poll workers do if the precinct register is missing? 
Contact the election supervisor immediately. Do not turn voters away from the polls. 
Have all voters vote a questioned ballot until a precinct register can be provided. 
• Ask each voter to sign in on the questioned voter register. If all lines are filled, 
have voters sign in on a separate sheet of paper. Voters must print and sign their 
names and provide identification.  
• Ask each voter to complete a questioned ballot envelope.  
If a precinct runs out of questioned ballot envelopes, voters may still vote. A plain 
envelope may be used. Ask voters to print their name, residence,  mailing address, and 
an identifier, such as a social security number, date of birth, or voter number, on the 
envelope. The voter must sign the envelope, and a poll worker must sign the envelope 
as a witness.  
• Ask the voter for identification. If the voter does not have identification, write “no 
ID” on the envelope.  
• Issue the voter a ballot and a secrecy sleeve or a piece of paper to return the 
ballot. The voter should be instructed to return the ballot to the poll worker in the 
sleeve or wrapped in paper after voting.  
• When the voter returns the voted ballot, it should be placed in the questioned 
ballot envelope or a plain envelope that has been written on and signed by the 
voter and a poll worker. Seal the envelope and have the voter drop the envelope 
in the ballot box.  
• When the election workers open the ballot box after the polls close, DO NOT 
open these envelopes. Rubber band the ballot envelopes together. Put them in 
the envelope for questioned ballots and mail or return to the election supervisor.  
 
61 
 
Polling Place Not Available 
What should the poll workers do if the polling place is unavailable? 
Find another polling place. If a school, public building, church or even a private home is 
nearby, ask permission to set up the polling place.  Post signs at both the original and 
new polling places so that voters can find the new location. 
As soon as possible, contact the election supervisor. The election supervisor will notify 
the voters and the news media of the change.  
Ballot Box Missing or Unusable 
What should the poll workers do if the ballot box is missing or unusable? 
A cardboard box or a similar empty box can be used as a temporary ballot box. Tape 
the box closed.  Cut a slot in the top to make an opening large enough for ballots to be 
inserted.  
Fire Alarm 
What should the poll workers do if a fire alarm goes off? 
Assess the situation; remember the safety of the poll workers comes first.  If possible, 
secure the ballots and lock the door as you leave. However, do not take any risk that 
may jeopardize the safety of your election workers.  
Tsunami Warning 
What should the poll workers do if a Tsunami Warning is issued? 
Follow evacuation instructions for your community provided by the public safety 
advisory for tsunami warnings. Assess the situation. Safety of your poll workers comes 
first. If possible, secure the ballots and lock the door as you leave. However, do not take 
any risk that may jeopardize the safety of your poll workers.

Language Assistance Requirements 
 
 The Voting Rights Act (VRA) of 1965 expanded the 14th and 15th Amendments to eliminate race-based 
restrictions on voting and to ensure that minority voters across the United States are given equitable 
access to the electoral process. It has since been reauthorized and amended on 5 occasions: 1970, 
1975, 1982, 1992, and 2006. 
 Section 2 of the VRA applies nationwide and identifies the following language minority groups as those covered by 
Section 203: 
• Asian 
• American Indian 
• Alaska Native 
• Spanish  
 Coverage under Section 203 of the VRA is triggered if more than 5% of voting age citizens are limited-English 
proficient OR if more than 10,000 voting age citizens are limited-English proficient, AND the rate of total voting age 
citizens that are limited-English proficient and have less than a 5th grade education is higher than the national rate, 
THEN the state, county, or county subdivision under consideration is covered for that specific language minority 
group.  
• For Alaska Native areas and other tribal lands, the determinations are made a bit differently: if more 
than 5% of Alaska Native voting age citizens belonging to an Alaska Native tribe are limited-English 
proficient AND the rate of those voting age citizens who are limited-English proficient and have less 
than a 5th grade education is higher than the national rate THEN any political subdivision(s) in which 
that Alaska Native area is located is(are) covered under section 203 of the VRA. 
• Within Alaska, Alaska Native Regional Corporations (ANRCs) can also trigger coverage under 
Section 203. However, ANRCs cannot themselves be determined for language coverage. Rather, if 
an ANRC meets the criteria for coverage, then the intersecting minor civil division or county is what 
becomes determined for language coverage.  
 Section 208 of the VRA requires that voters may receive assistance at any stage of the electoral process from a 
person of their choosing, as long as that person is not a candidate, their employer, an agent of their employer, or 
an officer or agent of their union.  
• This means that voters may bring a person of their choosing (other than those mentioned above) into 
the voting booth with them for assistance, should they require it. 

 
 
 
 
 
 
 How do language assistance requirements impact Alaska? 
  
 The State of Alaska is partially covered under 
Section 203 of the Voting Rights Act (VRA).   
 In compliance with Section 203 of the VRA and the 2021 Toyukak Second 
Stipulated Order, the Division provides language assistance to voters in the 
following areas: Aleutians East Borough (Yup’ik), Aleutians West Census Area 
(Tagalog), Bethel Census Area (Yup’ik), Bristol Bay Borough (Yup’ik), 
Dillingham Census Area (Yup’ik), Kodiak Island Borough (Tagalog), Kusilvak 
Census Area (Yup’ik), Lake and Peninsula Borough (Yup’ik, Aleut), Nome 
Census Area (Yup’ik), North Slope Borough (Inupiaq), Northwest Arctic Borough 
(Inupiaq), and the Yukon-Koyukuk Census Area (Gwich’in). Though not required 
by law, the Division also provides limited Spanish assistance in the Aleutians 
East Borough. 
 The VRA requires language assistance in voting and elections 
 This means we need bilingual election workers to serve on Election 
Day in areas of the state required to provide language assistance. 
 This means we must keep track of the number of voters requesting 
language assistance using the Language Assistance Requests Log. 
 
 
 
 
General Guidelines for Providing Language Assistance 
Follow these guidelines when providing language assistance: 
 
 Be courteous, sensitive, and respectful. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 Wear the “Can I Help You?” badge. 
 
 Don’t wait for the voter to ask for assistance. Provide “active” language assistance by greeting 
voters in your local Native language and asking them if they would like assistance. 
 
 Help the voter during each step of the voting process. You may go into the voting booth to 
assist the voter. 
 
 When providing assistance with ballot information, you may not voice your own opinion about 
candidates or issues appearing on the ballot. Each voter must make their own decisions about 
who or what to vote for. 
 
For ballot measures, you will need to provide a translation of the ballot measure text as it 
appears on the ballot. Use the translated sample ballot when providing assistance. You 
may not provide your own interpretation of the meaning of the ballot measure. 
 
 Using the Language Assistance Requests Log, keep track of the type of assistance provided. 
Record the type of assistance, not the voter’s name. For example “assisted with reading ballot”. 
 
 
 
 
 AND, REMEMBER: 
 
 Federal law allows a voter to have a person of his or her choice provide 
language or other assistance to the voter. 
 
 This person may go into the voting booth with the voter to assist them. 
 
 This person may be an election official, a family member, friend, bystander, 
campaign worker, etc. 
 
 This person may not be a candidate for office in the election, the voter’s 
employer or agent of that employer, or an officer or agent of the voter’s 
union.
