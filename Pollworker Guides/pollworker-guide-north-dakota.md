FOREWORD 
 
According to North Dakota Century Code (NDCC) § 16.1-05-03, the Secretary of State is required to provide an 
instruction manual detailing the responsibilities of election officials. This 2026  Election Officials’ Manual is intended to 
fulfill the requirements of the law by providing comprehensive instructions and procedures for election officials to 
look to for guidance in conducting elections in North  Dakota.

POLLING PLACES AND POLL WORKERS 
Polling Places: Upon a decision by the county commissioners, a county may utilize multiple precinct  polling places. 
County commissioners may also decide, if it is in the best interest of the county, to use one election board to 
supervise all or a portion of the precincts voting in the same polling place even if the precincts fall within different 
legislative districts so long as each district chairperson of each  qualified political party is given the opportunity to 
have representation on the election board if desired. (NDCC §§ 16.1-04-02 and 16.1-05-01) 
 
Inspectors: The county auditor, with county commissioners’ approval, shall appoint election inspectors, except in 
polling places containing city precincts only established by NDCC Chapter 16.1-04 where the       city governing board 
appoints the inspectors. The inspector selection is to be made on the basis of the inspector’s  knowledge of the 
election procedure. Appointments must be made forty days before the election. (NDCC § 16.1-05-01) Inspectors serve 
as the polling place supervisor. (NDCC § 16.1-05-04) 
 
Judges: The election judges for each precinct or polling place are appointed in writing by the district  chairs 
representing the two political parties that cast the most votes in the state’s last general election.    For special elections 
involving only no-party offices, the election official responsible for the administration of the election, with the 
approval of the majority of the members of the applicable governing body, shall appoint the election judges for each 
polling location. (NDCC § 16.1-05-01) The inspector or judges initial and deliver ballots to the voters and the judges 
perform other responsibilities  assigned by the inspector. (NDCC § 16.1-13-22) 
 
Composition of the Election Board: The election board for each precinct or polling place must consist  of an inspector 
and at least two election judges. (NDCC § 16.1-05-01) 
 
Clerks: The election official responsible for the administration of the election, with the approval of the  majority of the 
members of the applicable governing body, shall appoint the poll clerks for each polling     place. (NDCC § 16.1-05-01) 
Clerks manage the pollbook, verify voters’ identification and qualifications, update incorrect pollbook information, 
and perform other ministerial duties assigned by the inspector. (NDCC §§ 16.1-05-04 and 16.1-05-07) 
 
County Auditor May Appoint Judges if Forty Day Notice Not Received: If the district political party  chair fails to 
appoint the judges forty days before the election, the county auditor shall appoint the judges. If the county auditor 
has exhausted all practicable means to select judges from within the voting precinct and vacancies still remain, the 
county auditor may select election judges who reside outside of the voting precinct, but who reside within the 
precinct’s legislative district. If vacancies still remain, the county auditor may select election judges who reside 
outside of the legislative district but who reside within the county. (NDCC § 16.1-05-01) 
 
County Auditor May Request an Additional Judge: In polling places in which over one thousand votes were cast in 
any election, the county auditor may request each district political party chairperson  to appoint an additional election 
judge. (NDCC § 16.1-05-01) 
 
Part-Time Inspectors, Judges, and Clerks: The county auditor may appoint part-time inspectors, judges, and clerks 
to allow for sufficient coverage at each polling place to satisfy the requirements of  those positions. At least one 
inspector and two judges from the election board must be present on the premises of the polling place during the 
time the polls are open to prevent the occurrence of fraud, deceit, or other irregularity in the conduct of the election. 
(NDCC § 16.1-05-01 and 16.1-05-04) 
 
Qualifications of Election Board and Poll Clerks: Inspectors, judges, and clerks must be qualified voters of the 
polling places in which they are assigned to work unless the county auditor has exhausted all means to appoint 
election judges from precincts within the polling place according to NDCC § 16.1 - 05-01. No individual may serve as a 
member of the election board or as a poll clerk who has anything of value bet or wagered on the result of an 
election. (NDCC § 16.1-05-02)

Student Poll Clerks: A student sixteen years of age or older enrolled in a high school or college in this state is eligible 
to serve as a poll clerk if the student meets the following qualifications: 
• Is a United States citizen or will be a citizen at the time of the election; 
• Is a state resident residing in the precinct at least thirty days before the election; and 
• Is a student in good standing. 
 
A student appointed as a clerk may be excused from school attendance during the hours that the student is serving as a 
clerk, including training sessions, if the student submits a written request to be absent  from school signed by the 
student’s parent or guardian and by the school administrator and a certification from the county auditor stating the 
hours during which the student will serve as a poll clerk.  A student excused from school attendance in this manner 
may not be recorded as being absent on any date for which the excuse is operative. No more than two students may 
serve as clerks in a single precinct. 
 
An individual sixteen years of age or older having graduated from high school or obtained a general  education 
degree from an accredited educational institution is eligible to be appointed as a poll clerk if the  individual meets the 
qualifications stated above. (NDCC § 16.1-05-02) 
 
Candidates and Relatives Not Allowed on Election Board: An individual may not serve as a member of the election 
board or as a poll clerk if the individual is a candidate in the election at which the individual  is serving, or who is the 
husband, wife, father, mother, father-in-law, mother-in-law, son, daughter, son- in-law, daughter-in-law, brother, or 
sister, whether by birth or marriage, of the whole or the half blood, of any candidate in the election at which the 
individual is serving. (NDCC § 16.1-05-02) 
 
Removal of Judges or Clerks: If at any time before or during an election, it appears to a county auditor that any  
election judge or clerk is not qualified, the county auditor shall instruct the inspector to remove that judge or clerk at 
once and fill the vacancy by appointing a qualified individual of the same political party. If the disqualified judge or 
clerk had taken the oath of office, the inspector shall place the oath or affidavit before the state's attorney of the 
county. (NDCC § 16.1-05-01) 
 
Training Sessions for Poll Workers: Attendance at an election training session conducted by the county auditor is 
mandatory for members of the election board and for poll clerks. (NDCC § 16.1-05-03) 
 
Compensation of Poll Workers: The county shall pay at least the state minimum wage to the relevant election 
officials. Members of the election board and poll clerks who attend the training sessions must  be paid at least the 
state minimum wage for the hours in attendance in the session in addition to  necessary expenses and mileage. 
Members of election boards who attend the training sessions provided before primary and general elections must be 
paid at least 25% more than the state minimum   wage during the time spent in the performance of their election 
duties. Members of the election board and poll clerks who attend a training session within the six months prior to a 
special election must be paid at least 25% more than the state minimum wage during the time spent in the 
performance of their election duties in addition to necessary mileage and expenses. (NDCC §§ 16.1-05-03 and 16.1-
05-05)

ELECTION WORKER RESPONSIBILITIES BEFORE POLLS OPEN 
Failure of Election Board Member to Appear: If a member of the election board fails to appear for the opening of 
the polls, the remainder of the board shall select an individual to serve in the absent  individual’s place. If a judge fails 
to appear at the opening of the polls, the remainder of the board shall select an individual of the absent individual’s 
political party if such an individual is reasonably available. Any qualified individual without regard to political affiliation 
may fill the office of inspector or clerk. (NDCC § 16.1-05-02(5)) 
 
Oath of Office: Before assuming the duties, each member of the election board and each poll clerk  shall take and 
subscribe an oath in the following form: 
 
I do solemnly swear (or affirm as the case may be), that I will perform the duties of inspector,  judge, or clerk according to 
law and to the best of my ability, and that I will studiously endeavor to prevent fraud, deceit, and abuse in conducting the 
same. 
 
The oath may be taken before any officer authorized by law to administer oaths (e.g., county auditor, deputy county 
auditor, county commissioner, notary public). If no such officer is present at the opening of the polls, the inspector or 
election judges shall administer the oath to each other and to the poll clerks.   (NDCC § 16.1-05-02(3)) 
 
Inspector to Supervise Conduct of the Election: The election inspector shall supervise the conduct  of the election 
to ensure all election officials are properly performing their duties at the polling place.  The election inspector shall 
assign duties so as to equally and fairly include both parties represented on the election board. (NDCC § 16.1-05-
04(1)) 
 
Inspector to Assign Ministerial Duties to Poll Clerks: The election inspector shall assign duties to poll clerks, who 
shall carry out the duties assigned by the election inspector. (NDCC § 16.1-05-04(2)) 
  
Voting Booths or Compartments – Number Required: A sufficient number of voting booths or compartments shall 
be in the polling place to enable the elector to mark his or her ballot screened from observation. At least one 
certified tabulation device and ballot marking device must be provided in each polling place. (NDCC § 16.1-13-29) 
 
Voting Instructions to be Posted in Each Polling Place: Posters printed in large type containing instructions to 
voters on obtaining and preparing ballots and a copy of NDCC § 16.1-01-12 (Election Offenses) shall be posted in 
each polling place. (NDCC § 16.1-06-19) 
 
Complete Text of Measures and Educational Material to be Posted: At least five copies of the complete text of any 
constitutional amendment or initiated or referred measure must be provided to the polling location. One copy must 
be posted conspicuously in the polling place on the morning of the election. Additionally, educational material 
created and distributed by the Secretary of State related to statewide ballot measures shall be posted in the polling 
location.  (NDCC § 16.1-06-19 and NDCC § 16.1-01-18) 
 
Post Precinct Maps and Polling Location Hours: Four copies of a map showing the election precinct boundaries 
and the date and hours the polling location is open must be posted prior to the opening of the polls. (NDCC § 16.1-
06-19) 
 
Post Required Signage and Ballots: The election inspector shall post in the polling place one diagram of the voting 
system device and copies of the sample ballot. The election inspector and judges shall provide adequate instruction 
on the use of the voting system devices to each voter before the voter enters the voting booth. (NDCC §§ 16.1-06-17 
and 16.1-06-20) 
 
The required maps of the precinct, copies of the voter’s bill of rights, voting procedure and system diagrams, copies 
of election offenses, copies of complete text of measures and educational material, and diagrams of the voting system 
devices posted or displayed in the polling location must be readily available for review by the voter. Copies of official

Ensure Ballot Box is Empty: Before the polls open, the inspector and the election judges shall inspect the ballot box 
to make sure that it is empty. The ballot box must then be locked. While the polls are open, the ballot box must 
remain locked except as may be necessary to clear a ballot jam or to move voted ballots to a separate locked ballot 
box to make room for additional ballots. (NDCC § 16.1- 13-20) 
 
Flag Displayed in or Near Every Polling Location on Election Days: Federal law states that the United States flag 
should be displayed in or near every polling location on election days. 
 
Election Observers: Election observers must be allowed access to all stages of the election process, including early 
voting, absentee voting, vote tabulation, and recounts. Polling locations must be arranged in a manner that allows 
each election observer to plainly view and hear the occurrences of the polling location, without infringing on voter 
privacy. (NDCC § 16.1-05-09) 
 
Election observers must notify the election inspector of their intent to serve as an observer and must wear a badge 
with the election observer’s name and the organization, if any, they are representing. Election observers cannot serve 
in polling places where they are candidates on the ballot, may not wear campaign material, may not interfere with a 
voter or hinder or prevent the performance of the duties of an election official. (NDCC § 16.1-05-09)

VOTER ID 
Poll Clerks to Maintain the Pollbooks: A complete residential address for voting purposes includes a street, rural 
route address, city, state, and zip code. The use of a mailing address or Post Office Box number that is not the 
individual’s residential address is not sufficient for voting purposes. 
 
Voter Qualifications  
A qualified voter of the state is an individual who: (all must apply) 
• Is a citizen of the United States; 
• Is 18 years of age or older on the day of the election; 
• Is a resident of this state and of the precinct at least thirty days next preceding any election; 
• Is able to meet the identification requirements specified in NDCC sections 16.1-01-04.1 and 16.1-07- 06; and 
• Is not presently serving a term of actual incarceration as a convicted felon (NDCC § 16.1- 01-04 and NDCC § 
12.1-33-01). 
 
The following rules for voting eligibility also apply: 
• Every qualified elector of the state may only have one voting residence. 
• An individual’s voting residence must be determined in accordance with the rules for determining  residency 
as provided in NDCC § 16.1-01-04.2.  
o Every qualified elector may have only one residence, shown by an actual fixed permanent  dwelling, 
establishment, or any other abode to which the individual returns when not called elsewhere for 
labor or other special or temporary purposes. 
o The street address verified by the individual as provided in section 16.1-01-04.1 when requesting a 
ballot to vote must be the address of residence for the individual. 
o An individual retains a residence in this state until another has been gained. 
o The acts of residing at a new address for thirty days and updating the ID to be used for voting  with 
that address constitute a change in the individual's voting residence. 
• According to Section 2 of Article II of the ND Constitution, voting by individuals convicted and incarcerated 
for a felony must be limited according to NDCC Chapter 12.1-33. 
• No individual who has been declared mentally incompetent by order of a court (with specific  findings on the 
right to vote) or other authority having jurisdiction, which order has not been rescinded, shall be qualified to 
vote. (Section 2 of Article II of the ND Constitution, and NDCC § 30.1-28-04(3))

VOTER IDENTIFICATION AND POLLBOOKS (NDCC § 16.1-01-04.1) 
 
Voters Obtaining Ballots When a voter appears at the polling location, state law requires the voter to present valid 
identification to verify eligibility. Individuals may receive, vote, and cast a ballot by showing a valid form of identification 
with name, residential address, and date of birth to the poll clerks. 
 
1. Residential Address: A valid form of identification must include the individual’s name, residential address (a 
P.O. Box number is not acceptable), and date of birth. 
 
2. Acceptable forms of identification include a valid:  
1. North Dakota driver’s license or non-driver’s identification card. 
2. Tribal government issued form of identification. * 
3. Long-term care certificate (provided by ND facility) 
 
3. Supplemental documentation: If an individual's valid form of identification does not include all the information 
required or the information on the identification is not current, the  identification must be supplemented by 
presenting any of the following issued to the individual which provides the missing or outdated information: 
1. A current utility bill; 
2. A current bank statement; 
3. A check issued by a federal, state, local, or tribal government (including those issued  by BIA for a tribe 
located in North Dakota, any other tribal agency or entity, or any other document that sets forth the 
tribal member’s name, date of birth, and current North Dakota residential address); 
4. A paycheck; or 
5. A document issued by a federal, state, local, or tribal government (including those issued by BIA for a 
tribe located in North Dakota, any other tribal agency or entity, or any other document that sets forth 
the tribal member’s name, date of birth, and current North Dakota residential address). 
6. A printed document issued by an institution of higher education for an enrolled student residing in 
the state which must be accompanied by a student identification card issued by the institution and 
containing the student’s photograph and legal name. The printed document must  contain the 
institution's letterhead or seal, as well as the student’s:  
a. legal name;  
b. current residential address, and;  
c. date of birth.  
 
4. Voters without valid ID:  
1. If the individual’s identification does not verify his or her qualifications, or the individual is not able to 
show a valid form of identification, but he or she asserts qualifications as an elector in the precinct, the 
individual may mark a ballot that must be set-aside securely in a sealed envelope. (See Page 10) 
2. If an individual presents a North Dakota driver’s license or non-driver’s identification card and the 
license or card indicates the individual is a noncitizen, but the individual asserts valid citizenship, the 
individual may mark a ballot that must be set-aside securely in a sealed envelope. After the ballot has 
been set-aside, the individual may present an updated North Dakota driver’s license or non-driver’s 
identification card to the election official responsible for the administration of the election via print or 
electronic means before the meeting of the Canvassing Board 
 
5. Intent of ID Requirement: The purpose for this requirement is not to deter people from voting but to 
ensure that the voter is: 
1. Old enough to vote, 
2. A U.S. citizen, and 
3. A resident of the state and precinct in which he or she desires to vote. 
 
Qualified electors have the right to know that the votes are being cast by only those voters                  qualified to do so.

ELECTION WORKER RESPONSIBILITIES AFTER POLLS OPEN 
VOTING PROCEDURE 
1. As an individual enters the polling location and approaches the poll clerks’ table, the poll clerk shall ask for 
the individual’s name and identification. Valid forms of identification include:  
a. North Dakota driver’s license or non-driver’s identification card. 
b. Tribal government issued form of identification. * 
c. Long-term care certificate (provided by a North Dakota facility) 
 
If an individual’s valid form of identification does not include the North Dakota residential address or date of 
birth, or the North Dakota residential address is not current, the individual may  supplement the identification 
with a:  
a. current utility bill 
b. current bank statement 
c. check or a document issued by a federal, state, local, or tribal government (including those issued by 
BIA for a tribe located in North Dakota, any other tribal agency or entity, or any other document that 
sets forth the tribal member’s name, date of birth, and current North Dakota residential address);  
d. paycheck, or  
e. printed document issued by an institution of higher education for an enrolled student residing in the 
state and containing the institution's letterhead or seal, as well as the student’s legal name, current 
residential address, and date of birth (this document must be accompanied by a student 
identification card issued by the institution and containing the student’s photograph and legal name ) 
 
If the individual is not able to show a valid form of identification or if the identification provided does not 
verify his or her qualifications, but he or she asserts qualifications as an elector in the  precinct, the individual 
may mark a ballot that must be set aside securely in a sealed envelope.  
a. After the ballot has been set aside, the individual may validate his or her qualifications to vote by 
presenting an updated North Dakota driver’s license or non-driver’s identification card or other valid 
form of identification to the election official responsible for the administration of the election via 
print or electronic means before the meeting of the Canvassing Board. Once qualifications are 
validated, the Canvassing Board can approve the set-aside ballot for tabulation.      
 
2. After the poll clerk verifies the individual’s qualifications and the pollbooks are checked or updated, the poll 
clerk will direct the voter to an election judge.  
 
3. The poll clerk may need to update the voter record in the pollbook:  
a. If the name does not agree with the pollbook due to a name change, the poll clerk shall correct the 
pollbook if the individual is still a resident of the precinct. 
b. If the residential address does not agree with the pollbook due to the voter moving, the poll clerk 
may request proper supplemental documentation and correct the pollbook if the individual is in the 
correct polling place. 
c. If the individual has inadvertently come to the wrong precinct, the poll clerk is to direct the  individual 
to the correct precinct using the precinct finder or map provided by the county auditor for just such 
purpose. 
4. The election judge will initial a ballot, place the ballot within a secrecy sleeve, and instruct the voter on 
proper voting procedures.  
 
5. Ballot in hand, the voter is to move to an open poll booth and mark his or her votes in secret. 
a. The poll worker should make the voter aware of the ExpressVote ballot marking device, as every voter 
is welcome to use it. 
It is important to allow any voter who wishes to use the ExpressVote the ability to do so. Some voters 
may not want to divulge a hidden disability, such as illiteracy or dyslexia.

6. Voters may ask for a new ballot if they fill an unwanted oval by accident. 
a. Voters may request up to three ballots if they fill an unwanted oval by accident. The accidental 
ballots must be spoiled and the third ballot must be cast.  
 
7. After the ballot is marked, a voter may insert his or her ballot into the secrecy sleeve, bring it to the ballot 
tabulator, and insert the ballot into the machine for tabulation. 
 
8. If the ballot has been marked incorrectly, the machine will identify the problem and give the voter an 
opportunity for second chance voting. (NDCC § 16.1-13-23) 
a. The poll worker, standing at a discrete distance, may assist the voter (if requested) in either returning 
the ballot to the voter for second chance voting or in casting the ballot as is –  two ballots may be 
spoiled before the third must be cast as is. 
b. If the voter chooses to cast the ballot as is, the election judge must tell the voter that the  portion of 
his or her ballot marked with a conflict will not be counted. Conflicts that will be noted include: 
i. Crossover voting (Primary Only): When a voter votes for more than one (1) party on the 
ballot (front-side). This voids the front of the ballot but counts the non-party (back-side) of 
the ballot. 
ii. Over Vote: When a voter votes for more candidates than the contest allows. This voids the 
over voted contest, but all contests voted correctly are counted.  
iii. Blank Ballot: When a voter does not make any selections on the ballot. This increases the 
total ballots cast but does not give votes to any candidates.  
c. If the voter still desires to cast the ballot as is, the voter is to press the “accept” button on the ballot 
tabulator. 
 
9. The ballot is immediately counted by the ballot tabulator before it is deposited directly into the locked ballot 
box below. 
 
10. Throughout Election Day, there must be at least one election inspector and two election judges from  the 
election board in the polling place to prevent fraud and to maintain order; however, split shifts for election 
board members and poll clerks are allowed.

SET-ASIDE BALLOT PROCESS 
If an individual’s identification does not verify his or her qualifications, or an individual is not able to show  a valid 
form of identification, or an individual has identification that indicates they are a noncitizen, but asserts qualifications 
as an elector in the precinct, he or she may mark a ballot that must be set aside securely in a sealed envelope. 
 
1. Clerk’s Station: The clerk will: 
a. Confirm that the address provided by the individual is located in a precinct assigned to the polling  
place. 
b. Check the voter in on the pollbook as “Set-Aside”.  
c. Give the individual the orange set-aside envelope and secrecy envelope and instruct him or her 
to complete Steps 1 and 2 of the orange set-aside envelope. The individual may be asked to 
step aside until finished. 
d. Review the orange set-aside envelope for completeness and accuracy. 
e. Return the envelopes to the individual and send them to the judge’s station. 
 
2. Judge’s Station: The judges will: 
a. Complete Step 3 of the orange set-aside envelope. 
b. Issue the individual an appropriate ballot without initialing the ballot. 
c. Issue the individual a notice containing information on how, when, and where the 
individual may verify his or her identification. 
d. Inform the individual that after marking the ballot he or she is to: 
i. Place the ballot into the secrecy envelope, 
ii. Return to the judge’s station where the judges verify that the ballot is in the secrecy  
envelope 
iii. Seal the secrecy envelope in the orange set-aside envelope, 
iv. Return the sealed orange set-aside envelope to the judges, and 
v. Be reminded that the ballot will be counted by the canvassing board upon timely 
verification of the individual’s identification, per the information on the notice. 
e. Securely store the envelopes containing set-aside ballots for delivery to the election official 
administering the election after the polls close.  
 
3. Voter Verifies Eligibility to Cast Ballot: The individual may return to the office of the official 
administering the election for verification of their set-aside ballot or may submit the required identification 
or supplemental documentation to the official electronically. The election official administering the 
election will print or make a copy of the identification or supplemental documentation to include with the 
set-aside ballot. The ballot is delivered to the canvassing board for proper inclusion in or exclusion from 
the tally of votes.

OTHER ELECTION WORKER DUTIES WHILE POLLS ARE OPEN 
Pollbook Kept by Clerks of Election: The poll clerks will keep one list of the names of all individuals voting at each 
election. The list of names must contain the full name, including first and last name, the individual’s  identification 
number, and the complete residential address of each individual voting at the precinct. A complete residential 
address for voting purposes includes a street address, city, state, and zip code. The use of a mailing address or P.O. 
Box number is not considered sufficient for voting purposes. Each clerk will return the pollbook to the election official 
responsible for the administration of the election, which must be a part of the records and filed with other election

SECOND CHANCE VOTING 
Second-Chance Voting Prior to Casting Ballot: Ballots should be marked by the voter with a pen, to ensure choices 
or marks cannot be erased. The voter should not cross out unwanted choices or attempt to erase them. Instead, the 
voter should ask the election judge for a new ballot to mark his or her votes. A voter may receive up to two new 
ballots for these reasons, and the third ballot must be cast as is.  
Ballots returned to an election judge by an elector must be spoiled. 
 
1. Second-Chance Voting as Voter Attempts to Cast Own Ballot: After marking the votes on the  ballot, the 
elector may place the ballot back in the provided secrecy sleeve. The elector then will deposit the ballot in the 
ballot tabulator and  wait for confirmation that the ballot was tabulated and deposited into the ballot box.  
Voters requesting assistance may ask a judge or inspector to cast the ballot provided if the ballot is in the  
secrecy sleeve. If the ballot tabulator indicates the presence of a conflict and the possibility for second-chance 
voting, a poll worker, standing at a discrete distance from the  voter, will ask the voter if he or she desires help 
in either: 
a.  Having the ballot returned to the voter to mark his or her votes on a new ballot (the ballot with conflicts 
is to be returned to the judge to be marked as spoiled), or;  
b. Choosing to cast the ballot as is, even though it has conflicts.  
i. If the voter chooses to cast the ballot with conflicts, the judge must first inform the voter that 
the portion of the ballot with conflicts  will not be counted. (NDCC § 16.1-06-14(3) and NDCC 
§§ 16.1-13-23) 
 
2. Securing New Ballot Upon Spoiling of Others: A voter who chooses to have their ballot spoiled may obtain 
a new ballot , not exceeding three in total. The voter must give the election judge  the spoiled ballot before 
receiving another ballot to mark.  Each ballot returned must be spoiled immediately and, together with those 
not distributed to the  voters, must be preserved and secured in sealed packages and returned to the county 
recorder. (NDCC § 16.1-13-32)

• Knowingly vote in the wrong election precinct or district. 
• Disobey the lawful command of an election officer.

VOTERS WITH DISABILITIES  
ASSISTING VOTERS  
As a poll worker, you will be assisting a variety of voters, including those who may need accommodations to cast 
their ballot. Individuals with disabilities have different capabilities and preferences, and not all disabilities are obvious, 
so it’s important not to make assumptions about their disabilities or needs. Individuals for whom English is their 
second language may prefer to have an interpreter with them for voting. Some people m ay not feel comfortable 
sharing why they need an accommodation or assistance, and they are not required to disclose a reason. Here is 
guidance about assisting voters who may need accommodations in the polling location.  
Be Patient 
• Be considerate of the extra time it may take for an individual who is disabled or needs accommodations to 
perform tasks.  
• Give unhurried attention to an individual who has difficulty speaking or expressing their thoughts. Be patient 
and let the individual work to be understood without feeling rushed.  
Communicating  
• Make eye contact and always speak directly to the voter (even if they are with an interpreter, caretaker, 
companion, etc.) Keep in mind that your facial expressions, gestures, and body movements help in 
understanding.  
• Follow the voter’s cues to determine whether speaking, gestures, or writing are the most effective means of 
communication.  
• A voter who cannot speak or has English as a second language can give his or her name and address simply 
by providing identification to the poll worker. The poll worker will read the name and address aloud or write 
it on a piece of paper, and the voter can provide physical confirmation (such as nodding his or her head) 
that the information is correct.  
• Always use people-first language, which puts the person before the disability. Examples include phrases 
such as “the woman who is hearing impaired,” as opposed to phrases that identify people based solely on 
their disability, such as “the disabled man.” 
Offering Assistance 
• Show extra courtesy by offering assistance to try to make a voter’s experience easier. For example, if 
someone is experiencing fatigue while standing in line, you could ask if they would like a chair while waiting.  
• Ask before you provide help to someone, and respect that some individuals may not want assistance. Do not 
insist – take “no” for an answer if that is the voter’s wish. They know what their needs are and what 
accommodations work best for them.

• Do not push or touch another individual’s wheelchair or equipment without consent. People using adaptive 
equipment often consider the equipment their personal space.  
• When greeting a voter who has a visual impairment, identify yourself and state that you are an election 
official. If offering walking assistance, offer your arm to the voter, and tell him or her when you are 
approaching inclines or steps or turning right or left. Let the voter know when you leave his or her presence.  
• When giving directions to navigate the polling place, be as specific as possible and point out obstacles in the

Assistance in Voting Booth 
• Any voter with a disability or requiring extra assistance may be accompanied by, and receive assistance

Service Dogs

• People using service dogs are not required to provide proof of need.  
• A service dog does not have to be on a leash or wear a vest, but should always be under the handler’s 
control.  
• Do not pet or otherwise distract a service dog without permission from the owner.  
• If a voter has a service dog, walk on the opposite side of the voter from the dog.  
ASSESSING POLLING LOCATION ACCESSIBILITY 
(Note: This is all taken directly from the inside of the Polling Location Accessibility Guide)  
Parking 
• Every parking lot should have at least 1 accessible space for every 25 spaces. 
• There should be a minimum of 1 space marked van accessible. 
• Two accessible spaces adjacent to each other can share one van aisle between them.  
• Permanent and temporary accessible spaces must be clearly marked. Signs must include the universal 
accessibility symbol and a $100 fine for unauthorized use notice. 
Pathways 
• All indoor and outdoor pathways should be relatively level. Check for slopes, small cracks, potholes, and 
other problems that make access difficult. Fixing issues may include securely placing a rug over a defect or 
marking an alternate route. 
• All paths of travel must be clear and at least 36” wide. 
• Paths must have an area to turn around that is 5’ square. 
• Thresholds must not exceed ¼” unbeveled or ½” beveled. An unbeveled threshold is squared on the sides, 
while a beveled threshold is sloped. 
• Carpet must be short and flat, and all rugs and mats should be down securely or moved out of the way.  
• Objects hanging from the ceiling or protruding more than 4” from the wall must be marked with a cone 
underneath them to be cane detectable. Examples: overhead sign, water fountain.  
• Floors should be kept as dry as possible on rainy or snowy days. 
Entrances 
• Entrances/doorways must be at least 32” wide 
• Door handles must be 34 – 48” from the ground and operable with a single, closed fist from a sitting or 
standing position (no doorknobs). Doors should open without force. If a door is heavy, consider propping it 
open or posting a greeter to open the door for all visitors. 
• Entrances need 18” of clear wall space on the pull side of the door next to the handle. Mobility aids like a 
wheelchair or crutches need the space to get close enough to the door. 
• If there are stairs at the main entrance, there must be an alternate, accessible entrance available which must 
be unlocked and free of clutter. 
• Guiding signs must be visible from the main entrance to the accessible entrance. If your polling place is in a 
building with several routes through it, be sure that sufficient signs are in place to direct an individual to the 
most accessible route to the polling location.

• Open polls at the designated time. 
AFTER THE POLLS OPEN 
• Clerks keep and correct the pollbook as voters are checked-in to vote. 
• Ask each voter to show valid identification. 
• Ask each voter if they are a United States citizen , at least 18 years old , and whether they have resided in the 
precinct at least thirty days. 
• Direct voters to correct polling location if in wrong place. 
• Provide instruction and assistance to voters, including information about the ExpressVote. 
• Inspector or judge to distribute ballots to voter within a secrecy sleeve. 
• Warn voters to check for initials. 
• No write-in stickers allowed. 
• Warn voters not to split votes between parties in the primary election. 
• Provide additional ballots (up to two) if voter spoils a ballot. 
• Allow voters to place own ballots in ballot tabulator. 
• Do not allow ballots out of the polling place before polls close. 
• Allow voters standing in line at closing time to vote. 
 
AFTER THE POLLS CLOSE 
• Run canvass reports/results report of the votes from the ballot tabulator. 
• Compare total ballots cast on the ballot tabulator to the pollbook.  
• The election board must sign the generated canvass reports. 
• In primary elections, copies of the reports must be given to each political party, if requested. 
• Wrap and return ballots to county recorder as instructed. 
• Reports, pollbooks, oaths, and other required materials delivered to the county auditor.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS 
What do I do if a voter cannot or will not provide identification? 
 
Answer: If the individual asserts qualifications as an elector in the precinct, they may be issued a set-aside 
ballot. The set-aside ballot will only be tabulated by the canvassing board after the individual provides 
needed information to prove qualifications to the appropriate election official. 
 
What do I do if a voter asks for help with voting? 
 
Answer: If the ExpressVote ballot marking device is not an option the voter chooses to  use, then the two 
election judges from opposite political parties may assist the voter. Assistance is to be limited to reading the 
ballot to the voter, marking the voter’s choices for the contests, and depositing the voter’s ballot into the 
ballot scanner if the voter is unable to perform this function on his or her own or if the voter would rather 
not complete   this part of the voting process. A voter may also choose to have a friend or relative assist the 
voter in reading, marking, and/or casting his or her ballot so long as this individual is not a  candidate on the 
ballot, a relative of a candidate, the voter’s employer, or the voter’s union representative. 
 
What do I do if a voter asks me the names of write-in candidates for an office? 
 
Answer: Inform the voter that he or she is free to write the name of anyone the voter so  desires as a write-in 
candidate, but that some contests may require specific forms to be filed by the candidate in order for write-in 
votes to be counted on the candidate’s behalf and the law does not permit election workers to divulge 
information about candidates to voters in the polling place. Remind the voter to darken the oval next to a 
write- in candidate. 
 
What do I do when I am running low on ballots? 
Answer: Call the county auditor, city auditor, or school business manager (whichever is  appropriate for the 
election) as soon as you believe you might run short of ballots. 
 
What do I do if an individual is disrupting the polling location? 
Answer: If the individual is causing a serious disruption (your judgment is needed here) and the individual will 
not leave when requested to do so, you may call law enforcement and ask for assistance. You may also call 
the county auditor, city auditor, or school business manager (whichever is appropriate for the election) and 
ask for assistance. 
 
What if we notice some posters hanging in the polling place on Election Day for a candidate ? 
Answer: Remove any candidate posters in the polling place prior to the opening of the  polls. 
 
What time will the auditor bring lunch? 
Answer: The auditor will set the policy for lunch. 
 
What do I do if someone brings an absentee ballot and turns it in at the precinct? 
Answer: The law does not allow you to accept an absentee ballot from a voter at the polling location. 
However, you may tell the voter that you would be willing to spoil his or her absentee ballot and allow the voter 
to vote a new ballot at the precinct. You must remember to make a note of this for the county auditor’s 
records. 
 
What do I do if I need to use the restroom? 
Answer: Restroom breaks are allowed.
