Meal and Break Periods   
• Board workers may bring snacks and lunches, but they should not be on table 
where the voters are signing in or receiving ballots.  
• Every effort should be made to limit meal breaks to 30 minutes.  
• As a group, your team should discuss who will go at what times, and 
communicate throughout the day if you are taking restroom breaks, going for a 
short walk, smoking, etc.  
• Keep in mind when “surges” of voters will show up (before/after work, at lunch).  
• The busiest time at the polls is from 5:00 p.m. until the polls close, so arrange to 
have all breaks completed before this time.  
  
Wages (§32-227) 
• Judges and Clerks are paid minimum wage per hour.  
• Inspectors are paid 60 cents above minimum wage per hour, mileage, and a 
stipend for supply pick-up and return.  
• Board workers who complete the required training will be paid for the average 
length of the training session. 
• Election Board Coordinator requires your Social Security # to process payroll. 
• You may decline your wages by ﬁlling out Wage Waiver Form (request via email) 
 
Payroll Procedure 
• Use standard 12-hour time notation, NOT military/24-hour. 
• Inspector will have each board worker note an emergency contact person on the 
payroll form before the polls open.  
• The Inspector will note the arrival and dismissal times of each worker, and if a 
board worker takes a break signiﬁcantly longer than 30 minutes.  
• Payroll form will be time stamped at the Election Ofﬁce when the Inspector 
returns supplies, plus 10 minutes added to allow for additional returns to the 
warehouse. 
• Time away from the polls other than lunch must be approved in advance by the 
Election Board Coordinator unless it’s an emergency

Civil Leave (Time Off) for Election Duty (§32-241) 
According to Nebraska statute, no employee missing time to serve as a poll worker 
shall be subject to: 
 Discharge from employment 
 Loss of pay 
 Loss of sick leave 
 Loss of vacation or personal time 
 Threat of any action or penalty 
 Any other form of penalty 
• Election Work is considered “Civil Leave” in the same category as Jury Duty.  
• If you have ANY issues regarding employment protection, please reach out 
immediately to our ofﬁce or the Secretary of State’s Election Division. Our ofﬁce 
can send a letter to your employer verifying your service if requested.  
 
Early Voting for Poll Workers  
• Everyone who serves as a poll worker is added to the Permanent Absentee List. 
You will receive a postcard before every election to request a ballot be mailed to 
your home. 
• Board workers who are not assigned to work at their home precinct, and all 
reserve/substitute board workers, are encouraged to vote Absentee, or in person 
at the Election Ofﬁce ahead of election day. 
• Your early vote ballots must be returned to Election Commission before election 
day. 
• You may NOT return your voted ballot with the precinct’s voted ballots.  
 
Dress Code 
• Clean and neat attire is requested 
• Electioneering is prohibited for board members. No party, candidate, or issue may 
be represented or promoted – overtly or implied. 
• Remember that you will be sitting for long periods of time and need to be 
comfortable doing so. 
 
Conduct 
• Work as a team to accomplish all objectives and treat each other respectfully. It’s 
a long day, so we need to remember that we are there to serve our voters together. 
• Treat all voters with courtesy, respect and patience. 
• Avoid asking personal questions of a voter unless required by election duty. 
• TVs, radios, and newspapers are not allowed because of political advertisements. 
• Refrain from excessive and loud visiting with voters and among board workers. 
• Do not engage in ANY political conversations while conducting the election. 
• Cell phones should be silenced and put away while voters are present. 
 
 
 
8 
 
Before Election Day 
• You will be contacted in the last couple of weeks leading up to the election by the 
Inspector of your polling place to conﬁrm that you are still planning to serve on 
election day.  
• You may ask them questions at that time, especially if you are new to election 
work or are assigned to a new polling place.  
• Often folks will want to know where to park, what door to use to enter, whether 
you will have access to a kitchen at your location, and so on.    
 
What to bring 
• We recommend bringing meals and snacks, along with water or other beverages, 
although you may have fast food nearby that you can take advantage of instead.  
• We suggest a phone charger/cable that plugs into a wall outlet, in the event that 
you need to look up a lot of voters during the course of the day.  
• We also suggest some things to do during slower periods if you like, such as 
books or magazines, crossword or puzzle books, a handheld video game, knitting 
or crochet, etc.  
• We do not permit outside employment work, or anything messy or too distracting 
from the purpose of the day – the election.  
 
Election Board make-up and worker requirements (§32-221 to §32-226) 
• The team of workers stafﬁng a polling place on election day is called an election 
board.  
• A board consists of 3-5 board workers/poll workers: an Inspector + 1 or 2 Clerks 
(different parties) + 1 or 2 Judges (different parties), depending on the 
anticipated turnout for the given election. Boards are doubled at some precincts. 
 
Requirements to serve as a Board Worker 
• Must be a registered voter in Lancaster County. 
• May not be a candidate on the ballot. 
• Must be able to read, write, and speak English. 
• Must have good eyesight and hearing. 
• Ability to follow instructions and write legibly. 
• Ability to sit from 8:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. 
• Ability to set up and take down the voting area and lift and assemble voting booths 
and other equipment with the help of other workers. 
Inability to meet any of the requirements may result in removal from election board. 
 
 
Positions and Duties 
9 
 
Conflict of Interest 
• If a board worker or immediate relative is a candidate on the election’s ballot, 
please notify the Election Ofﬁce before Election Day.  
• To avoid the appearance of impropriety, the board worker may be asked to sit out 
that election or may be assigned to a different precinct. 
 
Inspectors, Clerks and Judges 
• Inspector – election board worker in charge of the polling place, supervises 
Clerks and Judges, picks up and returns supplies, frequently ﬁlls out provisional 
paperwork with voters, and generally oversees all activity on election day. 
• Clerks – election board workers responsible for greeting and checking in voters, 
verifying current address and photo identiﬁcation, and capturing voter’s 
signature. Also knowing what to do in cases of name changes, Absentee voters, 
lack of ID, and changes of address. 
• Judges – election board workers responsible for the ballot “chain of custody” at 
the polls on election day, signing ballots to identify them as ofﬁcial, handing out 
correct ballot styles to voters based on address and/or political party, giving 
instructions to voters on how to correctly ﬁll out and return ballot, and receiving 
voted ballots back, placing them securely in locked ballot box.  
 
Party balance and Non-Partisan workers (§32-223) 
• As much as is possible, members of each election board should be balanced 
between members of the parties casting the most votes in the county.  
• A board worker who is registered Nonpartisan (NP) is not afﬁliated with any party 
and can serve in any party afﬁliation capacity. 
 
Training (§32-228 & 32-235) 
• It is required that ALL board workers complete a paid training session before 
every statewide election (even years).  
• New workers will be asked to complete training regardless of which election 
cycle it is.  
• Failure to complete training may result in removal from the election board.  
• Training is conducted online. You will receive an email with a link to the videos to 
watch and quizzes to complete.  
• Quizzes are scored automatically and need an 80% or better to be considered 
“passing”. You may re-take any that you do not score high enough on. 
 
Exchange of Duties  (§32-226) 
• Judges and Clerks can exchange any and all duties and should be cross-trained to 
do so. 
• Position changes during the day are at the discretion of the Inspector. 
• Individuals with limitations should discuss these with the inspector before 8:00 
a.m. on Election Morning. If possible, the Inspector will make accommodations or, 
if needed, call the Election Ofﬁce. 
10 
 
Other election officials and workers (§32-225) 
• District Inspector - election worker who visits multiple precincts on election day 
to evaluate set-up and processes / procedures, answer questions, and provide 
extra supplies as needed. District Inspector directly represents the Election 
Commissioner, and ALL board workers are required to take their direction. 
• ExpressVote Technician - election worker who visits multiple precincts on 
election day to ensure ExpressVote machine is at the correct location, is 
operating correctly, and that poll workers have set up and test-voted the machine 
correctly. 
• Temporary Staff – election workers hired to serve at the Election Commission 
ofﬁce in the time immediately surrounding an election. It is very likely that you 
will be speaking to a Temp if you call the ofﬁce on election day, who will take your 
information and issue, and pass it on to a permanent staff member who will 
reach out to correct the issue. 
 
Poll Watchers (§32-1525) 
• Observers / Poll Watchers – people from political parties, speciﬁc interest 
groups, or other organizations, observing the activities in a polling place.  
• They must present credentials from the Election Commissioner and must sign in 
on the Poll Watcher Register page, found in the blue book. They will have a 
lanyard and name badge.  
• Board workers should contact the Election Ofﬁce if there are any issues with 
observers.  
• If the observer has a question or concern, they should speak with the Inspector or 
contact the Election Ofﬁce. 
 
Poll watchers shall: 
• NOT interfere with any voter casting (or preparing to cast) a ballot 
• NOT prevent any election worker from performing their duties 
• NOT help a voter unless SELECTED by the voter to do so. 
• NOT engage in electioneering while at the polling place. 
• MAINTAIN A DISTANCE OF 8 FEET from the sign-in table, sign-in register, polling 
booths, ballot box, and any ballots which have not been cast (supplies table). If 
the room is not large enough to maintain the minimum distance, then you must 
post the sign stating the minimum distance (found in the blue book) they must 
maintain instead. 
 
Media Requests 
• You may answer questions from the media regarding the number of people who 
have voted. If the media persists in asking additional questions, please have 
them contact the Election Commissioner at (402) 441-7311.  
• TV cameras are allowed if they are not disturbing the voters or the voting 
process. Permission should be secured before anyone is ﬁlmed or photographed.

Exit Polling (§32-1525) 
• This is the process of asking voters as they leave the polling place how they 
voted. It is not permitted within 20 ft of the entrance to the polling place, or, if 
inside the polling place building, within 100 ft of any voting booth.  
• Anyone conducting exit polls should have credentials issued by the Lancaster 
County Election Commissioner, or by the Nebraska Secretary of State. If they do 
not, you may ask them to leave to obtain them.

The Ballot Box (§32-909) 
• The ballot box is loaded and secured with 2 chain of custody seals by Election 
Staff prior to election day. 
• It must remain sealed until it is opened and unpacked at the polling place on 
election day. 
• When the chain of custody seals are removed, they should be checked against 
the numbers listed on the Packing Slip to verify, and placed in small zipper bag in 
the Blue Book. DO NOT THROW SEALS AWAY. (See sample Packing List below.) 
• As items are taken out of the ballot box, a poll worker should initial the packing 
slip in the “Removed” column.  
• Once emptied, it must be veriﬁed as such by poll workers, closed and locked with 
padlock, and the key in the possession of the Inspector until polls are closed. 
• When supplies are checked in and seals veriﬁed, place the Packing List in the 
same zipper bag as the security seals (in the Blue Book). Fold if necessary.

The ExpressVote 
• Federal law requires every Nebraska polling place to provide a device that allows 
voters with visual or physical limitations to mark their ballots in private.  
• The ExpressVote machine will mark the ballot for the voter once they’ve made 
their selections, and these ballots will be processed just like any other ballots.  
• Feel free to also offer it to voters who have difﬁculty standing to vote, who 
possibly have a grip-strength issue (making it hard to hold the pen), or who have 
a palsy disorder (where the tremors might affect an oval being ﬁlled in 
completely, while still staying inside the line).   
• Voters do not need to be impaired to use the ExpressVote, but we certainly want 
it available for voters who cannot cast their ballot privately any other way. 
• When the chain of custody seal on the travel bag is removed, it should be 
checked against the number listed on the Packing Slip to verify and be placed in 
small zipper bag in the Blue Book. DO NOT THROW SEAL AWAY. 
• You will have video training on how to set up the ExpressVote machine, and 
printed instructions with the machine itself. It is important to follow the 
instructions with care to ensure that it operates correctly, and that it is kept in 
good condition. 
 
Other supplies  
• You will also have “loose” supplies like cardboard privacy screens and signage to 
display. The Polling Place set-up on Page 14 will tell you what to do with them. 
 
Precincts and Polling Places 
• Precinct (voting precinct) – geographic area of the county, determined by 
political districts like School Board, Legislative, or NRD.  
• City precincts are denoted by a number/letter combination such as 08C06 or 
12F17, and rural precincts are named by the location, like Waverly North (WAVN) 
or South Pass (SPASS). 
 
• Polling place – the school, church, community center, non-proﬁt, or business 
where registered voters go to cast their ballots on election day.  
• Must be ADA compliant, have adequate parking for crowds, be able to provide 
enough space to ensure privacy for voters, and be usable from early morning 
until late night on election days.  
• Generally located within the precinct boundary, some are actually located inside 
other precincts when no other suitable space is available.  
• Often, more than 1 precinct will vote in the same polling place, but the boards 
remain separate, including their check-in process and returned ballots.

• Contact the person indicated at that time to make arrangements to get into the

• Be sure to keep contact information handy on election day in case of emergency

Setting up the polling place 
Outside of the building:  
• Polling place adequately identiﬁed with “Vote Here” signs and arrow signs to help 
guide voters, plus Voter ID and Curbside Phone # signs on stakes 
• Accessible and identiﬁable handicapped parking 
• Political and campaign signs at least 200 feet away from the polling place doors 
(see more detailed information on Electioneering on Page 17)  
 
Inside the voting area: 
Inspector will direct the team members to the tasks needing to be done, but basically, 
we need to 1) set up the booths, 2) hang up all of the posters, 3) set up ExpressVote, 
and 4) arrange tables and supplies for board workers. Arrange voting area in the most 
efficient way possible, keeping in mind access to power for voting booths, and traffic 
flow so things move logically from start to finish. 
 
1) A minimum of 4 voting booths needs to be set up, side-by-side, with the ﬁrst one 
being plugged into a wall outlet and the rest daisy-chained from that one. Booths 
should be set up past the sign-in table. Check to ensure that each has a page of 
instructions taped up inside it. (Use wire tags on any booths needing repair.) 
 
2) Corrugated signs with wire stakes go outside (Voter ID and Curbside Assistance). 
There is a full list on Page 11 of the other posters and notices to hang, but they 
include things like precinct and polling place maps and lists, samples of 
acceptable/not acceptable identiﬁcation, required HAVA information, and the 
NEW combined voter information poster. Don’t forget the ones in the ballot box. 
Use the blue painter’s tape or the push pins in your Supply Tote, as needed. If 
possible, keep posters grouped relatively close so that voters don’t have to look 
in a lot of different places for the information. (Exception is the direction arrows 
that you may need to put down hallways to direct voters to the right location.)

3) Set up a table with a privacy screen for voters needing/wanting to sit down – it 
can share a table with the ExpressVote if there’s enough room for privacy. Set up

Taking down the polling place 
1) Disassemble voting booths; be sure all pens are removed from under blue 
boards, and any damage or non-working issues are noted on wire tag attached. 
Stand cases near the door or take directly to Inspector’s vehicle. 
 
2) Gather all signs and posters from outdoors, including wire stakes. Fold “bag” 
signs and put into labeled zipper bag. Take down all other posters and notices 
indoors, being very careful to take tape off without tearing, and re-folding along 
original fold lines. Place signs back into same labeled zipper bag. We re-use the 
vast majority of these from election to election, so it is important to treat them 
with care! Put all other supplies back into their labeled baggies as you found 
them in the morning. 
 
3) Unplug and shut down ExpressVote machine and return to the carrying bag. 
NOTE: power plug and brick should be in outside pocket of bag, NOT inside 
where it may damage the screen! 
 
4) Process paperwork, count ballots and ﬁll out Ballot Log Card. Instructions for 
these processes are on pages 32-33 with Inspector Instructions.

General Guidelines 
 
Electioneering (32-1524) 
Electioneering means the deliberate, visible display or audible or physical dissemination 
of information for the purpose of advocating for or against any candidate or measure on 
the ballot. Prohibited information includes a candidate’s name, likeness, logo or symbol, 
or a measure’s number, title, subject matter, logo or symbol. This includes buttons, hats, 
pencils/pens, shirts, signs, stickers or literature and drawings. No person shall do any 
electioneering or circulate petitions within 200 feet of any polling place, or any building 
designated for voters to cast ballots. A person may display yard signs on private 
property within 200 feet of a polling place or building designated for voters to cast 
ballots if the property is not under common ownership with the property on which the 
polling place or building is located. 
Weapons in the polling place (§28-1202.01) 
Per Nebraska statute, concealed weapons are NOT permitted in any polling place during 
an election. Additionally, Lincoln Public Schools (LPS) has a strict "No Weapons" policy 
on their properties, whether concealed or open carry. The City of Lincoln similarly 
prohibits all weapons on City property, including buildings, libraries and parks. Other 
polling places not covered by the above regulations are at the discretion of the property 
owners, for example, places of worship or apartment clubhouses.  
 
Cell phones (§32-1527) should be silenced while in the Polling Place, but they are 
permitted to be used in the voting booth for voter research or to take “ballot selfies”. 
They may not be used to photograph other voters or poll workers without their 
permission.

Times polls are open, managing the line, and minimum workers required 
• Polls shall remain open from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. (32 -908) Those persons who are 
inside the polling place or standing in line by 8 p.m. must be allowed to vote. 
• If there is a line to vote, check to make sure the voters are at the correct precinct.  
• The voters may use a ballot sleeve or a table as a writing surface if the voter does 
not wish to wait for a voting booth.  
• Children are allowed to observe the voting process ; however, if they become a 
distraction to other voters, politely ask the parent or guardian to remove them. 
• A minimum of two board members must always be present  when the polls are 
open (§32-911).  
 
 
The standard voting process - (For Statewide Partisan Primaries go to Page 40) 
This is the heart of what you will do for MOST of the day during an election. There will 
be exceptions and situations requiring additional procedures, but the vast majority of 
voters will follow these steps. 
 
STEP 1 – Voter checks in with Clerk 1 
• Greet the voter and BEFORE asking for or accepting any identiﬁcation, ask “May I 
have your name please?” 
• Using the information they have verbally given you, ﬁnd their name in your 
alphabetical List of Registered Voters (located in the Blue Book). You may also 
ask for spelling clariﬁcation, middle initials, or date of birth to help determine if 
you are looking at the correct entry. (Some parents and children even have the 
same name, so we have to be sure we’re choosing the correct person.) 
• Then ask “Will you please conﬁrm your address?” This is very important: we DO 
NOT read their address to them or look at the address on their ID and match it. 
They are required to say it to us while we look at the list to see if it matches. 
This is an identity veriﬁcation step, so please do not skip or ignore it! If they do 
not wish to say it out loud, you may pass them a slip of paper and a pen for them 
to write it down, but either way, they must provide the address unassisted on 
their own. 
Standard Voting Procedure 
19 
 
• Once they give you the correct address, PRINT their name in the empty box next 
to the bar code. (This printed name tells the workers back at the office which bar 
codes to scan to update voter records.) 
 
 
 
• Now you can ask for their identiﬁcation, and you are ONL Y looking at the name 
and photo on the ID to conﬁrm they belong to the voter in front of you. (See Voter 
ID ﬂip book for more details on name and image accuracy requirements.) Verify 
name and photo and return ID to voter. 
• At this point the voter moves on to Clerk 2 for a signature. Ask Clerk 2 which Line 
# they will be signing in the Sign-In Register and write the same number in the 
ﬁrst column of your List of Registered Voters. There is already a small number 
printed in the box, but the one you print is to make it easy to cross-check the 2

GENERAL INSTRUCTIONS FOR INSPECTORS 
   
The Inspector is a deputy of the Election Commissioner and, as such, oversees and is 
responsible for properly conducting elections at the precinct. It is the duty of the 
Inspector to enforce the laws relating to elections, to see that all proceedings are in 
accordance with the instructions and the laws, and to be responsible for supervising 
board workers and informing the Election Office of any personnel not performing their 
duties adequately. The Inspector is also in charge of transporting all election supplies

Packet” to each Inspector containing specific information about each precinct and the 
list of board workers for each election.

GENERAL VOTER REGISTRATION INFORMATION 
     
 Eligibility (§32-312) 
 Any United States Citizen who: 
 
• is a resident of Lancaster County  
• will be 18 years of age or older on the first Tuesday after the first Monday 
in November in the current calendar year 
• has not been convicted of a felony, or, if convicted, has completed their 
sentence for the felony, including any parole term, and  
• is not found to be mentally incompetent by a court.  
 
Registration Sites  
 Online at www.nebraska.gov/apps-sos-voter-registration 
 The Election Ofﬁce at 601 N 46th Street in Lincoln, or for additional locations call 
them at (402) 441-7311.  
 Registration is also offered when obtaining a driver’s license or State 
Identiﬁcation Card and at many state government agencies. 
 
Registration Deadlines (§32-302 & §32-321) 
 Mail-in registrations must be postmarked on or before the third Friday before an 
election.  
 In-person registration can be taken at the Election Ofﬁce up until 6:00 p.m. on the 
second Friday preceding an election. 
   
When is it necessary to re-register? (§32-314) 
 Voters must re-register if they change their name, address, or political party 
afﬁliation.  
 A voter who has moved within the County and has not re-registered falls under 
the provisional voting statutes and is allowed to vote at his or her new polling 
place on Election Day. See page 35-37 for more information on provisional voting. 
 Failure to vote in a previous election does not require re-registration.  
   
 
Residence – for voting purposes (§32-116) 
 By law, the voter’s residence is the place where the voter has established a home, 
where the voter is habitually present, and to which, when the voter departs, they 
intend to return.  
 Temporary moves like military service, school attendance, or short-term out of 
town job assignment do not require a change of residence for voting purposes.

VOTING BOOTH GUIDELINES: 
 
 A voter must be offered the use of a voting booth but is not required to use one. 
 A voter may not occupy a voting booth for more than 10 minutes. (§32-921) 
 A voter may not remain within the polling place for more than 20 minutes 
(outside of voting and waiting in line). 
 If a voter is unable to enter the polling place due to illness or disability, they may 
request that a ballot be brought to them at curbside. (See Pg. 25-26 for Curbside 
procedure.) 
 If a voter needs assistance, they may ask a friend or relative, or board workers, to 
assist them in marking their ballot. (See Pg 25 for more details.) 
 Voters requiring assistance are exempt from time restrictions. 
 Each voter receiving a ballot shall vote before leaving the voting area. If a voter 
opts not to vote after receiving a ballot, insert the unvoted (but initialed) ballot 
into the ballot box.  
 If any person takes a ballot from the voting room, the voter loses their right to 
vote at this election and they may be found guilty of a Class V Misdemeanor. If a 
voter leaves the polling place with a ballot, make a notation on your Ballot Log 
Card, including the time, and call the Election Ofﬁce.

BALLOT MARKING GUIDELINES: 
 
 If the initials of two judges do not appear on a voted ballot, it must be rejected in 
front of the voter, marked "REJECTED" by a judge, and placed in the Rejected 
Ballot Envelope. Issue the voter a new ballot. (§32-916) 
 If the voter makes a mistake they should write "VOID" on the ballot and return it to 
a judge, who will insert it into the Spoiled Ballot Envelope, making every effort to 
preserve the secrecy of the ballot. Issue the voter a new ballot - not to exceed 4 
ballots total. (§32-917) 
 When instructions say, "Vote for up to/only X number of candidates",  
 X is the maximum number of ovals that can be blackened in that race.  
Voting for MORE than permitted will invalidate all choices for that race.  
Voting for LESS than permitted will not invalidate choices for that race. 
 Voters are not required to vote for every race on the ballot. 
 A voter may write in a name only when a line is provided, and they must also 
blacken the oval next to the name they write in. (§32-1005)
